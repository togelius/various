I worked from my kitchen table for most of 2021, and the thing nobody warned me about was the chair. We had four of them, bought at an IKEA in Malmö in 2014, and every one is designed for a forty-minute dinner. By March my lower back hurt so badly that I started taking calls standing at the counter, laptop propped on a stack of cookbooks.

The work itself went fine. Better than fine, some weeks. Without the commute I got about ninety minutes back each day, and I spent most of them sleeping. My manager, Priya, noticed I was answering emails at 7 a.m. and told me to stop, which I did not.

What I missed was overhearing things. In the office I'd learn that the billing migration was slipping because Tom sighed loudly at his desk. At home I learned it three weeks later from a slide deck. You can't schedule a sigh.

I'm back in the office three days a week now. I'd go four if they fixed the coffee machine on the fifth floor, which has been broken since October and has a handwritten sign on it that just says "no."
